# CS2 Spatial Demand & Land Economy Rebuild — Design Document

**Status:** Proposed — companion project to the Trip Simulation Rebuild
**Scope:** Replace Cities: Skylines II's demand, migration, land value, rent, building-leveling, and trade-pricing systems with a spatially disaggregated economy under Georgist land accounting, shipped as a code mod sharing infrastructure with the routing rebuild (see `cs2-trip-simulation-rebuild.md`, referenced below as "the routing doc").

---

## 1. Background

### 1.1 How the vanilla systems work

**Demand.** Two demands exist, one hidden: a hidden *household demand* controls whether new households spawn at outside connections, while the visible bar is *building demand* — construction pressure on zoned land. Both are global scalars computed from citywide factors (average happiness, homelessness, residential tax rates, open education spots, open jobs), with household wealth and size mapping to density preference (families → low density, students and singles → high). Vacancy feedback is citywide: vacant homes anywhere suppress demand everywhere. Buildings spawn on eligible zoned cells roughly uniformly once building demand is positive — site selection is only weakly steered by desirability. The one genuinely spatial step is allocation: an existing household picks a specific home by scoring rent against income, commute, services, and pollution. Population can keep growing with an empty demand bar, since arrivals fill already-built vacant stock; the bar gates construction, not growth.

**Migration.** Households materialize at outside connections at a rate set by the global factors against an implicit fixed outside world — a rate-limited flow from an infinite bath, with no field dynamics, no depletion, no migration memory.

**Rent and land.** Post-Economy 2.0, the "virtual landlord" is removed; occupants pay building upkeep which is absorbed into building condition, with rent computed as (LandValue + ZoneType × BuildingLevel) × LotSize × SpaceMultiplier. The location-premium component is destroyed on payment; no agent anywhere earns rental income. The launch-era land-value death spiral (building level feeds land value feeds rent, until occupants can't pay and buildings abandon) was patched by rebalancing, not by removing the circularity.

**Taxes.** Residential tax is an income tax bracketed by education level (education determines job qualification, hence wages); business taxes are per product type. All sliders run roughly −10% to 30%, and effects flow through exactly two channels: revenue, and a happiness/demand malus. There is no property or land tax of any kind.

**Trade.** Outside connections buy and sell at fixed prices with infinite depth. Freight is seller-delivered: the producing company's vehicles haul to the buyer (retail is the inverse — customers travel to shops).

### 1.2 Problems this creates

The global demand scalar is illegible (players cannot answer "if I zone X here, will it fill") and produces nonlocal effects — a vacant exurban strip suppresses downtown demand one-for-one. Construction ignores location, so building placement carries no information. The fiscal loop is severed: infrastructure investment raises land values, land values raise rents, rents are destroyed — the treasury never feels the player's best decisions, which is a structural cause of the "economy plays itself" complaint, alongside taxes that optimize to a threshold constant. Flat infinite-depth trade prices make monoculture export spam the dominant industrial strategy. Building levels grind uniformly toward maximum with no geographic structure. And vacancy, misuse, and speculation on well-located land are all costless.

### 1.3 Modding surface

Identical to the routing doc §1.3: managed ECS systems are replaceable wholesale (demand systems, spawn systems, budget systems are all managed); Harmony cannot patch Burst code but none of the hot paths here are Burst-critical; the maintenance cost is a fork of vendor systems, mitigated by the adapter discipline of routing doc §5. This project additionally *consumes* the routing rebuild's infrastructure: cached CCH access costs, nested-dissection cells, corridor dirty flags, Layer 3 destination buckets, and realized travel-time telemetry.

## 2. Problem Statement

Replace global-scalar demand with a three-tier spatial economy: (a) migration as rate-limited flow against a weakly endogenous outside world; (b) allocation of households and firms by market access with two-sided consistency; (c) local absorption where construction, leveling, and abandonment respond to residual submarket demand — all sitting on (d) a Georgist land-accounting core that closes the fiscal loop, (e) trade prices with finite depth and transport-cost parity bands, and (f) overlays that make every mechanism legible, answering the player's actual question: *if I zone X here, will it fill, at what rent, and what is it worth to the city?*

## 3. Constraints

**Shared infrastructure.** All access terms must come from the routing rebuild's cached CCH machinery at cluster granularity — no independent distance computations. Cluster keys are nested-dissection cells; refresh rides corridor dirty flags plus a slow sweep.

**No new agent classes.** No landlord class, no mortgages, no household balance sheets, no governance/voting models. Declined complexity re-enters only as parameters in existing systems (tenure hysteresis, hurdle rate, construction lag). Exit — moving out — is the sole expression of occupant preference.

**Circularity guard.** Every assessment derives from market-access bids computed over the population and firm distribution — never from a parcel's own realized rents. This severs the vanilla death-spiral feedback by construction, and it is an invariant, not a tuning choice.

**Anti-synchronization.** No mechanism may coordinate mass behavior on a shared clock: assessment anniversaries are staggered per household, redevelopment staggering comes from real composition heterogeneity (funding rates, within-segment moving-cost distributions), and no citywide reassessment or repricing event exists.

**Vanilla interop.** Building levels remain discrete 1–5 for rendering and milestone hooks (fractional progress is presentation only); all state round-trips through save/load; every tier is feature-flagged back to vanilla behavior independently.

**Deliberate open-economy boundaries.** Three phantoms are retained by design: the phantom developer (expected-zero P&L at hurdle rate h), the outside world (taps and drains), and the phantom bank (loans). Terminal household balances — the last member dying with no surviving household — escheat *outward* to the same national counterparty that pays transfer income, pairing the death-drain with the pension tap at one referent and denying the city any fiscal windfall from mortality. Everything else circulates.

## 4. Proposed Solution

### 4.1 Tier A — Migration margin (global, slow)

Household inflow per tick = elasticity × (city attractiveness − outside utility), lagged, and asymmetric: out-migration responds more slowly than in-migration (attachment and loss aversion), giving boom/bust asymmetry. Attractiveness is the Rosen–Roback bundle — wages, rents, and amenities — evaluated per segment, so housing stress automatically damps arrivals through the rent term (the principled version of vanilla's 1.1.8 rule that reduces new arrivals while homelessness is high). Outside utility is weakly endogenous through three slow scalars: a **reservation threshold** that rises with cumulative net in-migration against a region-size parameter (drawing down the migration field) and replenishes over time; **prominence**, which widens the effective field with city size (large cities recruit beyond their region); and **network memory**, a decaying stock of past inflow that lowers effective moving costs for the next arrivals (chain migration momentum). The region-size parameter is a map/difficulty knob shared with trade depth (§4.5) — one "how big is the world out there" setting governing both people and goods. Firm entry runs symmetrically on aggregate residual profit. Arrival-constrained behavior downstream (departure times, absorption pacing) inherits the routing doc's adaptive machinery.

### 4.2 Tier B — Allocation (market access)

All spatial scoring uses access weights w(p,q) = e^(−θ·c(p,q)) on cached CCH costs with mode-appropriate θ. Consumer access per segment s (income × education × lifecycle) sums jobs by tier, goods, schools, and amenities, minus pollution and noise — with lifecycle carrying real weight structure: seniors have exogenous transfer income, near-zero job-access weight, and high amenity and healthcare weights (retirement geography emerges from the bid formulas rather than being scripted), while students and singles bid low with high density tolerance. Firm-side terms: **commercial** capture of household spending via a phantom entrant inserted into the Layer 3 logit against incumbents (the bucket machinery run hypothetically), minus freight-in (best of local price + haul or import parity), wages, and opex; **industrial** output priced at the best of local buyers or exit parity net of haul (Weber location behavior — weight-losing industries near sources — falls out of the input-haul term), with pollution coupling negatively into neighboring residential access; **office** with near-exogenous output price and the spatial terms on the input side — educated labor access and an agglomeration factor A(p)^γ over nearby offices (γ ≈ 0.05–0.1), which makes office demand self-concentrating.

All claims pass through **doubly-constrained balancing** (a few Sinkhorn/IPF iterations on the cluster-level access matrix) so that jobs claimed equal jobs available and workers claimed equal workers available. What the system then carries everywhere is **residual demand**: unfilled positions and unserved spending after incumbents *and after the pipeline ledger* (§4.6). Residual, not gross access, is what predicts whether a new building fills — and it is what the demand bars display, retained as per-type aggregates.

**The bottom of the market is defined, not implicit.** A household whose income cannot cover its assessment follows the insolvency pipeline: cut consumption, then re-sort down the price gradient into cheaper submarkets — which genuinely exist here, since charging S on V makes decayed low-level stock inexpensive (§4.3) — then emigrate if it can fund the move, and otherwise enter a sheltered homeless state with real shelter capacity, from which re-housing draws on the same allocation machinery. The floor state couples back to Tier A through the rent term of attractiveness, and the bucket-based property search removes the truncated-queue bug class that produced vanilla's stuck homeless populations.

### 4.3 Tier C — Land accounting (the Georgist core)

Every occupied unit's payment decomposes as **S + tax + wedge**, uniformly assessed — no per-person pricing, no preference aggregation; heterogeneity acts only through sorting and timing.

**Structure charge** S = (h + δ + m) · V, where V = condition × RC (replacement cost) and h is the single hurdle rate used everywhere. Charging on V rather than RC means run-down buildings are cheap to occupy — accepted realism, and it makes the decay spiral self-consistent. Paying S holds condition (the sinking fund funds continuous renewal); underpayment decays it.

**Land value** is assessed net of conversion: the flow LR(p) = max over *permitted* configurations u — including "current use at declining condition, saving δ" as a candidate — of [Bid_u(p) − S_u − a(h,L) · remaining transition cost], where remaining transition cost = (ΔRC for renovation-in-place; demolition + RC_new − salvage of V for scrape) **minus the parcel's escrow balance**. Capitalized value for assessment and display: P_L = LR / (r + τ_L) with r = h (the standard tax-capitalization formula; at full capture the market price of land approaches zero — there is deliberately nothing to speculate on). One annuity operator a(h,L) is the only lump↔flow bridge in the system, used in exactly three places: the structure charge, the conversion deduction, and write-offs.

**Taxes** are split-rate: τ_L on land value (default: full capture — the treasury is the residual claimant on location value) and τ_S on structure value (default: **zero**; taxing improvements taxes exactly the behaviors the design wants, and the slider exists as a self-teaching policy experiment — raising τ_S visibly stalls upgrades and preserves parking lots). The parcel owes land tax regardless of occupancy: vacancy drains rather than pauses, which is the anti-speculation tooth. The income tax by education bracket is retained unchanged as the labor side, legible as a migration instrument.

**The wedge** = LR(p) − residual(current configuration) is in principle treasury revenue; by default policy it is **earmarked into the parcel's upgrade escrow** (a tax-increment-financing analog: incremental land revenue funds the development that realizes it), with a per-district toggle to pure-LVT general revenue — a real tradeoff, since un-earmarked districts lose pre-funding and redevelopment timing degrades to raw-margin triggers. Because the escrow balance reduces the assessed conversion deduction, the assessment **ramps itself**: as the bank fills, the wedge grows, so the ramp is convex — slow onset, accelerating finish, matching observed gentrification phenomenology with no phase-in parameter.

**Incidence emerges rather than being scripted:** taxes reduce disposable income → lower bids → lower land values and LVT base → shifted migration margin. The threshold-hunting exploit dies because the optimized object is no longer a constant.

### 4.4 Tier C′ — Leveling, redevelopment, and tenure

**Supported level** ℓ*(p) = argmax_ℓ [Bid_ℓ(p) − S_ℓ] is an interior optimum produced by convex costs (RC_ℓ = RC₁·γ^(ℓ−1), γ ≈ 1.4–1.6) against concave, segment-driven bid uplift — so the level map is emergent geography, not a grind. New construction spawns directly at the residual-maximizing level (hot submarkets spawn level 4; nothing decrees level 1).

**Renovation-in-place** (level changes within the footprint — what vanilla levels are) runs on a single funding clock: the escrow fills from the wedge across all units; at ΔRC the renovation fires with tenants in place; everyone re-anchors at the new cost basis; if demand has moved ℓ* further, the cycle re-arms. Fill time = ΔRC/(N·wedge) — the inverse rate of return on the increment, so hot frontiers fund fast because the gap *is* the return. **Downgrade is the same mechanism with the sign flipped:** where the assessment exceeds market willingness, vacancy and deficit underfund S, condition slides, and the building decays toward what the land supports — no separate decay subsystem. On demand collapse mid-fill, the escrow **drains into condition** (renovation as the consolation prize of stalled redevelopment), keeping the ledger honest.

**Scrape-and-rebuild** (density jumps, rezoning) additionally requires physical vacancy: once the bank is full and the gap sustained, vacated units stop re-letting (**warehousing**), with forgone rent as the natural brake — only large gaps justify the drain.

**Tenure and gating.** The upgrade fire condition follows the *owner tag*, not the building type: owner-occupied single-household structures get a consent/turnover gate (one household, no coordination problem — the escrow waits, pre-funded); rentals fire automatically. The owner tag is rare-to-absent on multi-unit buildings by scope — condo governance is deliberately unmodeled. Owner-tagged households carry larger moving-cost margins drawn from *distributions within segment* (real variation, not injected jitter) plus a small local-upkeep bonus; long-tenure demographics therefore genuinely resist redevelopment longer. A per-district **tenant-protection policy** reintroduces phased assessments for sitting tenants with new leases clearing at market — producing the empirically observed tenure discount and slowing redevelopment: the preservation-versus-development tradeoff as a dial rather than a script. Displacement under the default is gradual by construction: each household times its own exit as its assessment converges on the new basis.

### 4.5 Tier D — Trade and prices

**Each exit carries its own supply/demand law; there is no explicit multimodal composition.** Per (resource × exit), the sustained marginal price follows one functional family, p(Q) = anchor + t·(Q/ρ)^(1/d), with the exponent set by the mode's catchment dimension: **road exits, d = 2** — p = a + t·√(Q/ρ), the square-root law of an expanding two-dimensional catchment, marginal impact shrinking forever with no asymptote; **rail exits, d = 1** — p = a + t·(Q/ρ), linear, with a large terminal intercept (player-built cargo terminal, amortized only above a freight-volume threshold) and a shallow slope, because a corridor is a one-dimensional catchment with feeder-inflated density; **sea, d → ∞** — flat at global price + shipping + haul, capacity-capped, present only where the map provides it; **air, d → ∞ with a high anchor** — a deep-but-expensive bath, relevant only to goods whose value per kg clears the freight cost, and capacity-capped. Depth-relevant density ρ keys off the same region-size knob as migration (§4.1). Sustained position Q on each curve is an EMA of drawn volume; the fast **transient-impact layer** rides on top (burst orders move price briefly beyond the sustained position, resilience decays it back), and the slow **permanent layer** moves named physical parameters — induced transport capacity lowers t, regional development raises ρ, and player-built terminals add whole exits.

**Multimodal composition is emergent, not computed.** Companies already choose among **quantized offers** across all exits via the Layer 3 flexible-destination machinery (offer price as attractiveness, routed haul as access cost, one search) — and buying cheapest-first across independent curves *is* horizontal summation. Three regimes then follow with no scripting: at small volumes road wins outright (no intercept); at mid volumes rail wins (intercept amortized, shallow slope); at very large volumes the linear rail law *re-crosses* the square-root road law — a single corridor exhausts while the 2-D catchment keeps decelerating — which is the realistic saturation pressure that motivates a second corridor or the sea backstop. Within the mid band, both modes run simultaneously with marginal prices locked together and volumes splitting to hold the equality — textbook multi-source procurement, emerging from offer choice. The developmental arc (truck town → rail city → port metropolis) is a consequence, not a rule.

**Local prices** float inside a **parity band** [export parity, import parity] whose width is the actual routed transport cost to the relevant exit, including congestion — a traffic jam visibly moves the local price of concrete, downstream of the routing rebuild's soft-closure states. Exits into the same region share their sustained scalar (adjacent-exit coupling at the fundamental) with fast per-exit congestion deviations on top. Sustained raw exports feed back into Tier B as induced local processing demand (Weber pull plus an export-base multiplier on local-serving employment). Probabilistic waterway-port generation (Poisson ports along coasts/rivers with geometry-dependent exposure, demand-inducible hazard) is **map-generation flavor, not core mechanics**: a map either has a sea exit or it doesn't, with an optional demand-threshold event adding one.

### 4.6 Construction dynamics

Developer return per eligible (cell, permitted type) = predicted rent × predicted absorption (from residual demand and local vacancy) − construction cost, gated by the hurdle h; construction fires as a softmax over returns (logit spread — near-equivalent sites split rather than herd). Three disciplines are mandatory, not optional polish: the **claims ledger** decrements residual demand at commitment (later deciders see the pipeline, not the mirage — the cobweb overshoot self-meters); **construction lag** (instant building would delete the overshoot realism the ledger exists to contain); and **milestone re-evaluation**, abandoning projects whose expected return has fallen below remaining cost — stalled half-built projects in busts, emergent from existing machinery. A **calibration loop** tracks realized occupancy and rent of recent builds against the overlay's prediction *at their decision time* and folds the shrunk ratio back into predicted returns — this project's analog of the routing doc's exactness certificate, and the standing answer to model-versus-simulation drift: the target is measured, mean-reverting drift, not zero drift.

### 4.7 Overlays and legibility

The overlay is the build model rendered before building. Per zone type: expected rent/sqft, predicted time-to-fill, residual-versus-pipeline detail. Per parcel: supported level ℓ*, redevelopment pressure (the ℓ − ℓ* gap), and escrow fill fraction as a literal progress bar — the gentrification frontier as charging bars. Citywide comparator: **net fiscal yield**/sqft (revenue minus service cost — never gross, which teaches fiscal-zoning pathology). Per building, the price tooltip decomposes the payment into S / tax / wedge — "why is my rent high" answered with location versus structure versus neglect. Per resource, local price against its parity band and the current marginal offers. Demand bars persist as residual-demand aggregates.

### 4.8 Declined items

The rent-only paradigm rests on an identity worth stating once: **under full land-rent capture, renting and owning are economically identical** — home equity decomposes into structure value (whose upkeep the occupant funds while consuming its services; nothing to reclaim) and the present value of *untaxed* land rents, which full capture drives to zero. Capital gains on moves, imported basis for off-map arrivals (absorbed by the arrival-savings distribution), inheritance of home value, and starting basis for students all vanish simultaneously by construction rather than by omission. In the partial-capture regime (τ_L below full), uncaptured land rent accrues to occupants as an **occupancy surplus flow** — enjoyed while occupying, forfeited on exit, capitalized by no one, since no asset market exists — a deliberate deviation from reality (where it would price into homes) meaning the split-rate slider demonstrates speculation pathologies through vacancy and misuse costs rather than asset bubbles.

Mortgages, household balance sheets, and rent-vs-buy modeling (fail the timescale test — their real function lives on decade horizons the game compresses away; the gameplay-relevant residue, tenure stickiness and maintenance pride, ships as parameters). A landlord class and any preference-aggregation governance (rent-only closure; uniform assessment plus exit replaces voting). Structure taxation by default (exists only behind the split-rate slider). The housing-wealth consumption channel (below simulation resolution). A land/housing sale market (full capture drives land price to ~zero; there is deliberately nothing to trade, and no lock-in mechanics are needed beyond tenure hysteresis). Schedule-exact transit interactions inherit the routing doc's frequency-based declination.

## 5. Shared Infrastructure and Interface

This project is a second consumer of the routing rebuild's core, not a second core. It reads: CCH access costs (all Tier B terms), nested-dissection cells (cluster keys and the level-adaptive pairing), corridor objects and dirty flags (refresh triggers for access matrices and bucket-based capture), Layer 3 buckets (phantom-entrant capture, offer selection), and realized travel telemetry (commute terms in residential access). Its own adapters touch the vanilla zoning/parcel components, household and company spawn systems, budget/tax systems, and building level/condition — the same two-thin-adapters discipline as routing doc §5, with the same harness-first testability: benchmark scenarios should include a boomtown ramp, a demand collapse, a monoculture-export run, and a gentrification frontier, each with the overlay's predictions logged against realized outcomes. Every tier feature-flags independently back to vanilla behavior. Anti-synchronization invariants (§3) are asserted in the harness, not assumed.

Build order by risk and dependency: (1) Tier B access matrices + overlays in read-only mode (display predictions, change nothing — validates calibration against vanilla behavior); (2) Tier D trade scalars and parity bands (self-contained, kills export spam); (3) Tier C accounting with τ_L initially at zero impact (shadow assessment, logged not levied); (4) construction rewiring (§4.6) against live residuals; (5) Tier C′ leveling/escrow; (6) Tier A migration endogeneity. The game stays playable at every stage, and each stage's overlay honesty is measurable before the next stage trusts it.

## 6. Acceptance Targets

Vacancy localization: a vacancy shock in one district shifts construction within that district at ≥10:1 versus distant districts (vanilla ≈ 1:1 by construction). Level geography: the realized level map correlates with access (rank correlation against ℓ*) rather than uniform max-grind. Fiscal loop: a transit investment measurably raises LR revenue along its corridor within a few sim-days, visible in the budget panel. Trade: sustained monoculture export bends the marginal received price ≥30% below the flat-price baseline at equal volume; with a terminal built, the truck→rail→backstop mode progression emerges by volume in the harness's export ramp, and concurrently active exits show marginal-price equalization within tolerance. Dynamics: boom/bust asymmetry present; no synchronized reassessment or displacement events (per-household exit times form a distribution, not a cliff); stalled construction appears in engineered busts. Overlay honesty: predicted-versus-realized fill ratios converge to a stable band under the calibration loop, and the shrunk correction factors remain bounded. Performance: all Tier B/C refresh work rides existing dirty-flag budgets — no new per-tick channel scaling with parcel count.
